## 1. Technical Viability

### 1.1 What is genuinely novel versus assembled

The product is not technically novel in any single component. It is an integration product: licensed market data, read-only exchange adapters, chain RPC readers, a domain-owned lot and valuation engine, and a React SPA. Every one of those has a known implementation path, and the build-vs-buy analysis already chose the correct boundary — buy data, integrate venues thinly, build the reconciliation and valuation logic that constitutes the differentiator. [build-vs-buy] That boundary is sound and reversible component by component through ports (`MarketDataProvider`, `FxProvider`, `ChainRpcProvider`, `ExchangeReader`, `CostBasisEngine`). [build-vs-buy]

The technical risk therefore sits almost entirely in the **platform constraints**, not in the product logic. **Seven platform constraints carry real uncertainty** (table 1.2, rows 1–7); the eighth row is a domain-arithmetic trap, not a fixed platform constraint, and the ninth adds localisation as a scope decision rather than a technical unknown. **Two of the seven are unproven rather than merely hard**: Supabase Vault access from Rust, and evidence that the 99.9% availability target survives the chosen single-cluster topology. **Two can sink the launch rather than the build**: market-data rights and the MiCA/UK scope opinion (section 4).

### 1.2 Hard-constraint assessment

| Constraint | Status | Assessment and required mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supabase Vault access from Rust | **Unproven — spike first; one access path is mandatory** | pgsodium is a Postgres extension; Rust has no native binding, so every access is SQL through `sqlx` against Vault functions or `vault.decrypted_secrets`. The fixed rule "one schema and one role per service, no cross-schema grants" makes a direct read impossible. **Both viable designs require the same platform-schema exception**, so the choice is not "wrapper versus no wrapper" — it is how the data key is used once the wrapper exists. **Access path:** a privileged role owns a `SECURITY DEFINER` function (one per service, or one parameterised by service name) in a `platform` schema, granting only `EXECUTE` to the service role and returning only the named secret references that service is entitled to. `vault` is a platform schema, not a service schema; record the exception explicitly. **Preferred key design:** envelope encryption — the service fetches a *versioned* key-encryption key (KEK) reference at boot through the wrapper, holds it in memory with `zeroize`/`secrecy`, and stores per-subject data keys wrapped by the KEK in its own schema. Crypto-shredding is deleting the per-subject wrapped key. **The boot-time read does not remove the wrapper dependency and is not sufficient on its own**: (a) the KEK must be versioned and refetched on a short TTL (for example 5 minutes) or on first decrypt failure, so rotation takes effect without restarting every replica; (b) a rolling restart must tolerate mixed KEK versions by trying current then previous; (c) a boot-time-only read makes crypto-shredding a policy promise until every replica restarts. **Bootstrap is circular otherwise**: a service's own Postgres role password and connection string cannot come from Vault before it connects — deliver those through the platform provisioning path (Docker/Swarm bootstrap secrets limited to connection material, never application credentials) and document it. **Unreachable Vault** must be a stated behaviour (fail closed for credential operations; read-only cached data may continue), not an accident. **Key backup versus crypto-shredding** is a genuine unresolved tension (open question 6). This is a week-two spike with an automated test that greps the log sink, traces and error output for the secret value. |
| One schema and one Postgres role per service, no cross-schema grants | **Achievable, with a real cost** | The rule is clear and enforceable, but it removes joins and foreign keys across domains. Every cross-domain read becomes HTTP, referential integrity becomes application-level, and every aggregate becomes a fan-out. The valuation hot path needs holdings (Account) + asset mapping (Registry) + prices and FX (Market Data) + portfolio assembly (Portfolio): four hops. **The p95 <2 s risk is not the hop count** — four internal hops on a private overlay are normally tens of milliseconds — it is N+1 fan-out, vendor calls on the read path, and worst-case retry stacking. **Mitigation designed in from the start:** Portfolio serves a materialised valuation snapshot recomputed by a **scheduled Job Orchestration poll of Market Data's HTTP API**, not by an event (there are no queues and no cross-schema reads). Request-time fan-out is reserved for an explicit user-initiated refresh. Every owning service exposes batch-by-ID read endpoints so callers cannot N+1. Cross-service writes carry idempotency keys and compensating reconciliation, because distributed transactions do not exist. C10 is the load-test evidence. |
| JWT validation in every service | **Proven pattern; discipline risk** | The gateway forwards the Supabase JWT unchanged and every service re-validates it. Correct and cheap, but it creates a per-service auth implementation that must not drift: JWKS fetch and cache with rotation and jitter, algorithm pinning (reject `alg: none` and algorithm substitution), `iss`/`aud` checks, clock-skew tolerance on `exp`/`nbf`, owner scoping from `sub`, and a distinct audience for the internal service token used by scheduled job runs so a job token can never be replayed as a user token. Extract this into one internal platform crate with one conformance test suite run against every service; do not let ten services each write their own. The gateway's in-memory rate limits and caches are **per replica**, so horizontal scaling silently multiplies the effective limit — acceptable at launch only because the enforcing limits (auth, credential add, manual sync) are moved to a shared counter (CON-031). [architecture] |
| Multi-source price reconciliation | **Achievable technically; commercially gated** | Deterministic source priority, a divergence threshold with an alert, and last-known-price fallback with a staleness badge is straightforward engineering, and it is what makes the >99% accuracy claim credible rather than asserted. [intent-statement] The blocker is licensing, not code: CoinGecko forbids redistribution, storage and derived valuations without consent, and a cross-source median is itself a derived value. [build-vs-buy][market-trends] CoinMarketCap's tiers include commercial-use rights, which makes **CMC the safer primary for valuation** and CoinGecko a display/attribution source or secondary check — the reverse of the original recommendation. Resolve this before it is baked into the schema. |
| Exchange read-only adapters | **Achievable; large and under-estimated — but not the largest line** | Five venues at 10–20 engineer-weeks is a large build item, but it is **not the single biggest**: build-vs-buy prices the SPA at 12–20 and chain readers at 12–20 engineer-weeks, equal to or larger. What is true is that the adapter line is the **least reliable estimate**: each venue differs in auth (HMAC versus CDP JWT), rate-limit shape (Binance meters per IP and escalates 429 to an HTTP 418 ban of two minutes to three days; Kraken meters per key with a decaying counter), pagination, and key-permission defaults. APIs also churn: retired `listenKey` streams and the Coinbase Pro to Advanced Trade migration both invalidated existing integrations. [build-vs-buy] Partner programmes are contractually gated (Binance.US starts at USD 1M average monthly volume), so assume no partner status at launch. Mitigation: incremental sync with bounded backfill rather than full history on connect, **one cluster-wide per-venue and per-IP token bucket** (Account and Market Data share one egress IP; per-service limiters do not close the ban risk), permission probes charged to the same budget, per-venue circuit breakers, adapter contract tests against recorded fixtures, a per-venue capability matrix, and a user-visible "partial sync" state so a ban degrades to a labelled gap instead of a wrong number. Add 30–50% to the adapter estimate. |
| 99.9% availability on single-region Docker Swarm | **Design-achievable only with a corrected topology; not yet evidenced — and contractually risky** | 99.9% monthly is **43.2 minutes in a 30-day month and 44.6 minutes in a 31-day month**. On one self-managed Swarm cluster in one region that requires: **three manager nodes each in a distinct failure domain** (not two managers, and not three managers split 2+1 across two zones — that loses Raft quorum when the zone holding the majority fails), at least three worker nodes, at least two replicas of every service spread across workers, `start-first` health-checked rolling updates, redundant ingress/load balancing, and a backup/restore path meeting RPO ≤24 h / RTO ≤4 h. **One Swarm cluster is one control plane and one region is one failure domain**; losing the manager quorum takes down every service, including a read-only cache. Supabase Auth is on every authenticated request and has no fallback, and Supabase's own availability sits inside the customer-visible number; a serial dependency on one provider caps product availability at or below that provider's SLA. **Verdict: the target is achievable only as 99.9% of product-attributable time with upstream-provider outages explicitly excluded, backed by a measured failover drill, an external synthetic monitor independent of both the VPS provider and Supabase, and a documented Supabase SLA — or the paid-plan promise must be restated with product sign-off. It must not be sold before that evidence exists.** A second cluster/warm standby is the only way to underwrite the full number; it is unfunded (open question 8). [architecture] |
| No message queues | **Acceptable at launch; define the ceiling** | Fire-and-forget HTTP with bounded retries plus scheduled reconciliation is a coherent substitute at launch scale, and it removes an entire operational component. The costs are real: no durable async, no backpressure, retry storms against a failing downstream, and best-effort domain audit events that can be lost (mitigated by local logging as fallback — recorded as a completeness limit, not an absolute guarantee). Every write path needs an idempotency key or version check. The design breaks specifically on fan-out — a single price tick that must notify many users must become a scheduled digest, never per-event HTTP. **Record a numeric reconsideration trigger** (for example more than 1,000 notifications/minute, reconciliation lag above 15 minutes, or more than 50,000 active users), so the decision has a threshold rather than a vibe. |
| Domain arithmetic and decimals | **Achievable; one boundary trap** | `rust_decimal` is correct end-to-end for money and domain math, with no `f64`. Its 96-bit mantissa caps at about 7.9×10^28, so a raw uint256 atomic balance above roughly 79 billion 18-decimal tokens overflows before normalisation. Parse atomic units as `u128`/bigint at the adapter boundary and convert to `rust_decimal` only after scaling. Canonical asset identity stores chain decimals, display decimals and the conversion rule; impossible balances raise a reconciliation state rather than rendering. |
| Localisation (launch locale set, translated legal text, localized notifications, RTL) | **Achievable; scope is a decision** | The engineering is routine (i18n catalogue, locale-aware date/number/currency formatting, RTL layout mirrors). The binding constraints are: (a) the launch locale set, which is **undecided** and must be fixed before the M1 copy freeze; (b) legally accurate translations of disclosures, consumer terms and the privacy notice per jurisdiction, which are new legal deliverables rather than engineering; and (c) notification templates, which are currently deferred with Notification (Q8), so localized notifications depend on the Reporting degraded path. RTL is not a CSS flip — it changes layout, icon direction and chart axis order. Budget line in 3.2, effort line in 2.1 and condition C16. |

### 1.3 Technical verdict

Every constraint is satisfiable with known patterns. The three that can sink the timeline are (1) the Vault access pattern, because it is the only unproven integration and it gates credential handling for the whole product; (2) the materialised valuation path, because it determines whether p95 <2 s survives the no-cross-schema and no-queue rules; and (3) the exchange and chain adapters, because their effort estimate is the least reliable number in the plan. The two that can sink the **launch** rather than the build are market-data rights and the MiCA/UK scope opinion (section 4). None of these is a reason to stop; all of them are reasons to sequence them first.

### 2.2 The service-count problem the 8-service framing hides

"Eight services plus gateway and SPA" maps to the pre-extension baseline: Identity, Account, Asset Registry, Market Data, Portfolio, Reporting, Job Orchestration, Audit. That set cannot deliver the intent's own success metrics. [intent-statement] Two services are on the critical path and are missing from the count:

- **Ledger.** Cost basis, realised and unrealised P&L, transfer pairing and the reconciliation queue — the stated differentiator and the basis of the >99% accuracy claim — live in transaction history, not in valuation. [competitive-analysis][build-vs-buy] Folding that lot engine into Portfolio is possible but reverses the architecture's explicit decision to keep it separate, and it couples the highest-churn domain to the read path.
- **Billing.** Free-to-paid conversion ≥3%, paid plans with 99.9% availability, and entitlements and quotas enforced server-side all require plan and subscription state. [intent-statement][architecture] There is no way to charge without it.

The target architecture in fact names **12 backend services plus gateway**, having added Ledger, Notification, Billing and Analytics, plus a Support Console. [architecture] Three scope sizes are therefore in play; the plan must pick one explicitly:

- **Option A — 8 services (thin):** cost-basis engine folded into Portfolio as a module; checkout handled by the merchant of record with entitlements read from a gateway cache rather than a service. Fastest, but weakens the differentiator and accumulates architectural debt that must be repaid before paid scale. Its saving is **about 2–3 engineer-months**, not a timeline class change; it does not make a 6-month date feasible.
- **Option B — 10 launch services (user-confirmed at Q6):** the eight plus Ledger and Billing. Notification, Analytics and Support Console are deferred to post-MVP as an **explicit BRD deviation approved at Q8**, with the degraded paths named: (a) in-app alerts plus mandatory email alerts implemented in Reporting because Notification is deferred; (b) product analytics from a consent-aware, PII-free event path in the web tier; (c) support through a time-boxed, break-glass read-only admin view behind the gateway. The deviation must be written into the approved scope, not left implicit. This preserves the two load-bearing decisions.
- **Option C — full 12 services plus Support Console (not feasible inside 9 months):** the complete target architecture adds roughly **4–7 engineer-months** over Option B on the plan's own per-service figures (a workspace is 1–1.5 engineer-weeks inside the 10–15 row, and build-vs-buy prices auth, billing/entitlements and notifications together at 5–9 engineer-weeks). The "12–16 engineer-months" figure in the earlier draft was inflated and is withdrawn. Option C still breaches the 9-month window at 5 engineers once the deferred services' own integration, support and analytics work is priced, but the delta is a scope decision rather than an impossibility.

**Recommendation: Option B is the approved plan (Q6), with the Q8 deferrals recorded as an explicit BRD deviation and the mandatory-alert, analytics-consent and support-access paths named.** If the organisation insists on Option A, record the architectural reversal and drop the P&L-depth and paid-plan claims.

### 2.3 Why "one service at a time" is right but serialises the critical path

The confirmed delivery shape — one service at a time behind the gateway — is the correct choice for this team size. It avoids the classic small-team failure of ten half-built services with no working end-to-end path, and it keeps the gateway contract stable while each service lands. The cost is that the critical path is genuinely serial, which is exactly why the engineer count matters: with 3 engineers there is one stream and no parallelism; with 4–5 there are two safe streams (backend services, and SPA plus adapters), which is what moves the plan from 9 months of thin delivery to 9 months of viable delivery.

## 4. Regulatory and Jurisdictional Feasibility

### 4.1 The product boundary is defensible but narrow

The intent deliberately places the product outside custody and execution: read-only connections, no keys, no transfers, no automatic trades, and a human-approved order intent that is never transmitted as an order. [intent-statement] **The legal basis must be stated correctly: CPB is a hosted, operator-run read-only aggregator, not non-custodial wallet software, so the Recital 83 "fully decentralised / non-custodial software" carve-out does not fit it and must not be relied on.** The defensible basis is that the product supplies **none of the MiCA Art 3(1)(16) services**: (a) custody and administration, (c) exchange of crypto for funds or other crypto, (e) execution of orders, (g) reception and transmission of orders, (h) advice, (i) portfolio management, (j) transfer services. Counsel must analyse each listed service by name, in writing, and state the residual Member-State interpretation risk. [market-trends]

Two activities remain live hazards:

- **Personalised advice and discretionary portfolio management** are licensed under Art 3(1)(16)(h)–(i) and (24)–(25). Drift alerts, rebalancing proposals and "what should I buy" copy drift toward this line quickly, especially in a retail product optimised for activation. Decision support must stay generic and factual: show the position, the drift and the option, never a recommendation addressed to the user. MiCA Art 66 also constrains marketing communications, so the copy-review checklist (NFR-LEG-04) is a release gate for every jurisdiction and every localised variant.
- **Reception and transmission of orders** is separately licensed under (e) and (g). An order intent is safe only as a **documented handoff to a licensed venue** — recorded, shown, exported, never routed or forwarded on the user's behalf. [competitive-analysis] The moment the product transmits an intent to an exchange, it is in scope. Venue deep links, affiliate/referral revenue and "trade now" call-to-action copy can also attract arranging/advice analysis under UK RAO 2001 Arts 25/53.

That boundary is technically enforceable because the product never holds exchange trade permission or user keys, and adapters are read-only by construction. It is **not** enforceable by architecture alone; it needs copy review and a design principle that survives product pressure. [market-trends]

### 4.2 Jurisdiction sequencing versus the launch date

The confirmed answer is EU (MiCA) and UK first, then US, with a gate before data collection and external counsel validating disclosures. [Q4] The UK half needs two separate analyses, and the earlier draft covered only the second:

- **The current UK regime applies now.** Section 21 FSMA 2000 and the FSMA (Financial Promotion) Order 2005 restrict cryptoasset financial promotions to UK consumers (the FCA promotions regime has applied since October 2023). RAO 2001 Art 25 (arranging) and Art 53 (advice) may catch order intents, venue deep links, affiliate/referral revenue and marketing copy. A UK launch attempt is a **current-regime** question, not only a future-authorisation question.
- **The future regime is a later gate.** Applications opened 30 September 2026; the regime is expected in force around 25 October 2027. **[unverified — secondary source; Compliance must verify the commencement order before C2b sign-off, first review 2026-12-15.]** A 9–11 month MVP starting in November 2026 lands between **August and October 2027** — just before, or at, the expected commencement. [market-trends] So a UK launch inside this plan happens under the current regime, but the window is narrow and the new regime becomes a hard gate at commencement, falling immediately after launch and needing budget in the follow-on plan.
- **Sequencing is an approval-gate decision, not an assessment default.** The UK is kept as a launch market (Q7), so **C2b** must cover both the current promotion/arranging position and the future authorisation regime, and UK marketing must be gated with onboarding. **C2c** separately covers US state money-transmission/MSB exposure and is required **before the US phase**, not before UK data collection. If C2b evidence or the legal cap cannot support a UK launch, the UK becomes a **pre-positioning market** — gated off, no marketing, no data collection — only by escalation to the approval gate and a recorded decision, not by slipping it into a risk response. The earlier RAID response that unilaterally switched the UK off is withdrawn (R-003).

The EU rules may also move during the build: grandfathering ended 1 July 2026 and the review consultations closed 31 August 2026 **[unverified — secondary source; Compliance to verify against the Official Journal, first review 2026-12-15]**. A single legal opinion dated at the start of the build is not sufficient; it needs a review point before launch. [market-trends]

The US is correctly sequenced last. CLARITY passed the House but failed Senate cloture in September 2026 **[unverified — secondary source; Compliance to verify legislative status, first review 2026-12-15]**, so there is no stable federal frame; **state money-transmission/MSB exposure** is the risk if an order intent is ever construed as transmission, and this needs a separate opinion (C2c) **before the US phase** rather than an assertion that read-only tracking is outside MSB scope. 1099-DA plus DAC8 and CARF create cost-basis demand that the product serves without filing. [market-trends]

**Data protection is a separate launch regime, not a footnote to MiCA.** The controller/processor map, lawful basis per purpose, DPAs and subprocessor register, EU Article 27 representative and UK representative (if the entity is not established in both markets), cross-border transfer mechanism (SCCs plus transfer impact assessment, IDTA/Addendum or DPF reliance) and the Supabase project region are all Must requirements (BRD-012 NFR-PRV-09/10/14, NFR-SEC-25; BRD-017 FR-PRV-03/06/07). The Supabase region cannot be changed in place **[unverified — confirm against Supabase's current project-region policy; Architect to verify before the M0 region decision]**, so it is an **M0 decision**, not a launch-week one.

**Verification status.** Statutory and vendor dates marked [unverified] are drawn from secondary sources. **Compliance** owns re-verification against the primary instrument or provider policy, with a first review due **2026-12-15** and re-verification before each gate (C2a–C2c, C8, C13). The Architect owns the two platform claims (Swarm maintenance status, Supabase region immutability).

### 4.3 Non-negotiable launch gates

1. **Jurisdiction and age gate before any personal data is collected.** Enforced at the edge (DNS/WAF or ingress) **before request logging and before any non-essential cookie**, and in a **Supabase "before user created" auth hook or a signup proxy** — because the SPA calls Supabase Auth directly and Supabase stores the email and IP before the gateway or Identity sees a request. A browser-side check plus self-declaration is bypassable and a post-auth gateway check is too late for BRD-002 AC-USR-11, BRD-017 AC-PRV-10 and NFR-PRV-12. Fail closed on unknown country; use an effective-dated, versioned counsel allowlist; define "EU/EEA" (confirm EEA incorporation of MiCA); block marketing, waitlists, support intake and analytics in blocked markets; audit every block; re-assess on VPN/travel/residence change; and include the age gate. Minimise and state the retention of the IP data the gate itself processes. [BRD-012 NFR-PRV-12, NFR-LEG-02; BRD-017 FR-PRV-18]
2. **DPIAs before processing, not after the first real user.** Wallet addresses and keyed hashes can be personal data; EDPB Guidelines 02/2025 make a DPIA necessary. The DPIA must also cover exchange credentials, support access, analytics/consent, audit logs, age gating, retention and cross-border transfers; Article 36 prior consultation applies if residual risk stays high. It is an **M1 gate before the first real user**, not an M5 afterthought; M0 is synthetic-only for exactly this reason. [market-trends; BRD-017 FR-PRV-03/13]
3. **GDPR/UK GDPR launch pack:** Article 6 lawful-basis mapping (contract for the service; consent for marketing and non-essential analytics; legitimate interests for security/audit; legal obligation for tax/audit retention), Article 30 records, processor DPAs and subprocessor list, EU/UK representatives, transfer mechanism per processor, retention and deletion automation reconciled with the **proposed** 5-year financial-retention and 12-month audit periods (proposed pending **BRD-017 OQ-004**; **not settled law**), a tested breach-notification path (72 hours; processor notification chain), and an Article 37 DPO assessment. [BRD-012 NFR-PRV-09/10/11/14, NFR-SEC-25; BRD-017 FR-PRV-06/07/15/20]
4. **Consumer-subscription compliance before paid plans open.** Auto-renewal disclosure, cancellation as easy as sign-up, refund/withdrawal mechanics, no dark patterns, and ADR/complaint information: EU Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU Art 6(1)(q), Art 8(2) ("order with obligation to pay" button) and Arts 9–16 with the digital-content exceptions; UK Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 regs 13–18 and the DMCCA 2024 subscription provisions (confirm commencement); member-state rules such as Germany §312k BGB and France L215-1; US state auto-renewal statutes and ROSCA at the US phase. Unfair-terms review (93/13/EEC; CRA 2015 Part 2) and E-Commerce Directive Art 5 trader information apply. **The merchant of record is not automatically the trader for consumer-law purposes**; counsel must confirm who owes the disclosure and cancellation duties. [BRD-012 NFR-LEG-08; BRD-016 FR-BIL-09/12, BR-BIL-03/10, AC-BIL-10; D-018]
5. **Accessibility.** The European Accessibility Act has applied to EU consumer services since 28 June 2025 **[unverified — verify transposition and scope per member state; Compliance, first review 2026-12-15]**. The presumption of conformity is **EN 301 549**, with WCAG 2.2 AA as the product commitment (D-016); the EAA also requires an accessibility statement and a feedback mechanism, and Art 4(5) may exempt microenterprises providing services (confirm the <10 staff and ≤€2m thresholds and the entity-size determination). Keep the external audit for market access, but do not assert a blanket statutory duty until counsel confirms the member-state implementation. Accessible PDFs need a PDF/UA path, not only WCAG. **This is a named gate (C13), not a footnote.** [market-trends; BRD-012 NFR-A11Y-01–05]
6. **Market-data, benchmark and exchange rights.** Written confirmation that every chosen source permits **display, storage, historical retention, redistribution and derived valuations (including cross-source medians)** in the launch jurisdictions; the same for **exchange API commercial-use and retention terms** (the cost-basis/P&L core stores full trade history), **benchmark indices**, and **RPC/indexer** terms. Maintain a licence register with evidence, attribution and review cadence. This is a commercial gate that blocks the same launch as the legal ones. [BRD-012 NFR-LEG-05/06; D-020; build-vs-buy]
7. **Operational readiness before public beta.** A public status page with component health and incident history (BRD-019 FR-SUP-09), SEV-1 communication within 15 minutes (NFR-AVL-06), a named primary/secondary on-call rota, runbooks for control-plane loss / node loss / Supabase down / disk full / certificate expiry (BRD-019 FR-SUP-20), and one rehearsed incident drill. A 99.9% paid SLA cannot be sold without them.

### 4.4 Regulatory verdict

Feasible, with the product boundary as the load-bearing element. The plan is not blocked by licensing, but it is blocked from launch until the market-data/exchange rights question, the **EU MiCA, UK current-regime and future-regime, and US state money-transmission opinions (C2a–C2c)**, the DPIA/transfer pack and the consumer-subscription terms are answered in writing. All are longer-lead than any engineering task in the plan and should start in week one.

### Open questions that can still change the verdict

1. **Do the chosen vendors permit storage, history, redistribution and cross-source medians?** CoinGecko's terms forbid storage as well as derived valuations; if no affordable vendor grants storage and derivation, the snapshot/P&L core cannot launch as designed. Largest single risk in the plan.
2. **Does MiCA treat a documented, untransmitted order intent as reception/transmission?** Counsel's answer decides whether the order-intent surface ships at launch or moves post-MVP behind a licensed partner.
3. **Is a UK launch lawful under the current regime, and does UK-facing marketing, affiliate/referral revenue or a venue deep link change that?** Determines whether the UK is a launch market or a pre-positioning market; if pre-positioning, the confirmed Q4 answer must be escalated at the approval gate.
4. **What is Supabase's actual SLA, and does PITR cover the RPO ≤24 h / RTO ≤4 h targets?** If not, the availability target and the paid-plan promise both move.
5. **Which Supabase region, and what is the transfer mechanism for every processor?** The controller's establishment, the EU/UK representatives and the region pair must be decided at M0; the region reportedly cannot be changed in place **[unverified — Architect to confirm against Supabase's current policy]**.
6. **How are Vault keys backed up without defeating crypto-shredding?** Restoring the database without the per-subject keys yields unreadable credentials; backing the keys up makes erasure a policy promise rather than a cryptographic one. A decision, not a default.
7. **What is the market-data/benchmark/exchange licence fee?** Until answered, the USD 150,000 envelope has an unpriced liability in it.
8. **Is a second cluster/warm standby or a second provider funded?** Single-cluster single-region keeps the control plane and region as single points of failure; the 99.9% target is materially safer with one removed, and the high-case budget does not contain it.
9. **Who operates the Swarm cluster on call?** There is no dedicated operations role in a 3–5 person team, and the decision to self-manage the orchestrator is explicitly accepted. [architecture] The plan must name the person or accept the availability risk; this is C11.
10. **Which indexer provides EVM token discovery, Solana and Tron at acceptable cost?** Log scanning and Tron coverage are the most underestimated lines after the adapters; Alchemy/Infura do not serve Tron, so a Tron provider must be named. The RPC overage allowance (up to USD 200/month) may not cover it.
11. **Is historical FX available at transaction timestamps?** exchangerate.host Business historical data is end-of-day; local-currency cost basis and P&L at transaction time need a stated source or an accepted EOD limitation.
12. **Which member-state consumer rules apply, and is the entity a microenterprise for the EAA?** Determines the consumer-subscription workstream and whether the accessibility duty is statutory or a market-access commitment.
13. **What are the exchange API commercial-use and retention terms per venue?** BRD-012 NFR-LEG-06 and D-020 make this a Must, and the P&L core depends on retaining full trade history.
14. **What are the benchmark index licence terms?** BRD-020 and NFR-LEG-05 require licensed benchmark and attribution data; no line is budgeted.
15. **Does the merchant of record take on the trader role for consumer law, or only tax remittance?** Determines who owes the disclosure, cancellation and withdrawal duties.
16. **What is the launch locale set, and does it include an RTL locale?** Undecided. Fixing a multi-locale launch adds 4–8 engineer-weeks and USD 5,000–15,000, and requires per-locale legal review rather than translation alone (C16).
17. **Is there an organisational change freeze, re-org or competing priority in the 9–11 month window?** None has been identified, but nothing on record confirms it; a freeze resets the schedule.
18. **Is any existing system integration expected at launch** (legacy data migration, enterprise SSO, accounting/ERP export)? The plan assumes none; if one exists, the effort ranges and the M0/M1 sequence must be re-estimated.
19. **Does any launch partner or channel require PCI DSS, HIPAA or SOC 2?** All three are assessed as not applicable for the consumer launch; an enterprise or partner commitment would add an audit programme not in this envelope.
20. **Are the 5-year financial-retention and 12-month audit periods settled law or still proposed under BRD-017 OQ-004?** Until settled, the retention/deletion design and the DPIA are provisional.
