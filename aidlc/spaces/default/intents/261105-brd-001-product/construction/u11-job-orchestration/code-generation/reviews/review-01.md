| R-01 | Critical | services/job-orchestration/infrastructure/src/lib.rs > HttpAuditClient::append and services/job-orchestration/application/src/scheduler.rs > Scheduler::execute audit action | The C10 payload cannot be accepted by the Audit service. | R-02 | Major | services/job-orchestration/application/src/scheduler.rs > queue_retries and MAX_ATTEMPTS | The retry bound is the crate constant `MAX_ATTEMPTS = 5`, not the job's `max_attempts`. The migration seeds `max_attempts` 2 and 3 for every job, so retries run past the configured bound. | R-03 | Major | services/job-orchestration/application/src/scheduler.rs > classify and services/job-orchestration/infrastructure/src/lib.rs > reap_expired | Retry timing ignores the schedule. `classify` returns `next_attempt_at = now + 1 hour` for every failure, while BR2.1 and the reliability design require one schedule window later, or 5 minutes for `every:` schedules under 10 minutes. `Schedule::retry_at` exists but is used only by a domain test. `reap_expired` instead sets `next_attempt_at = now`, so a reaped run is retried on the very next tick rather than under the same rule as BR2.2 requires. | Compute `next_attempt_at` from the job's parsed schedule with `Schedule::retry_at` for both the target-answer path and the reaper, and test an `every:5` job (5 minutes) and a `daily:` job (next day). | New |
| R-04 | Major | services/job-orchestration/application/src/scheduler.rs > Scheduler::execute | The run lease does not cover the call. The lease is set to `LEASE_SECONDS` (30 s), while the target client is handed a hardcoded `3_600` seconds and the definition's `timeout_seconds` (seeded 300–1800) is never used. WF1 requires the run lease to be `timeoutSeconds + 30`; with a 30 s lease a slower-than-30 s call can have its row reaped as `failed` by another replica while it is still in flight, its outcome then fails the ownership check and is discarded, and the attempt is retried. A hung target also holds the tick for up to an hour instead of the configured timeout. | Pass `definition.timeout_seconds` to `TargetClient::run` and set the run lease to that value plus 30 s, clamping both to the 1–3600 range the schema allows. | New |
| R-05 | Major | services/job-orchestration/application/src/scheduler.rs > Scheduler::tick | Pending runs are executed one at a time in a `for` loop with no concurrency bound. WF1 specifies at most 32 outbound calls in flight per instance. One slow target (seeded timeouts reach 1800 s) therefore stalls every other queued job, the retries, the reaper and the heartbeat for that whole time, and the 60 s ticker collapses into a burst of immediate ticks when the call finally returns. | Execute the pending page through a bounded concurrent executor (32 in flight, each bounded by its definition timeout) or schedule the calls outside the tick and record their outcomes asynchronously, as WF1 describes. | New |
| R-06 | Major | services/job-orchestration/src/lib.rs > tick supervisor and services/job-orchestration/application/src/scheduler.rs > Scheduler::tick | The heartbeat is written only at tick start and after every call in the tick has finished, so a tick that is executing a slow call leaves the shared heartbeat untouched. The functional specification's BR4.1 says every 30 s while in flight; the implementation never does. With the seeded timeouts and the serial executor, a healthy but slow tick pushes the heartbeat age past 180 s and pages on-call for a scheduler that is working. | Refresh the heartbeat while a tick is in flight (a small task that renews only while this instance holds the lease), or make the tick's work bounded so its duration cannot reach the 180 s dead-man threshold, and align the functional specification and rules text on which cadence applies. | New |

### Summary

The service's shape, Clean Architecture boundaries, schema, grants, idempotency key text, ownership-checked outcome writes and 202/409/4xx classification are sound and the unit's tests are green. It is not ready because the retry bound and retry window ignore the job definitions, the run lease and call timeout contradict WF1, the tick has no concurrency bound, two alert rules can never behave as designed, and the C10 audit payload is rejected by the audit contract, silently losing operator audit events.
